Asylum and Migration: digitalisation of case management in the area of return, readmission and reintegration

28 October 2026  |  New legislative proposal

What's next: Parliament's lead committee and the Council's working party examine it in parallel. No deadline applies at this stage; most files take one to two-plus years.

Official summary (Legislative Observatory, Legislative proposal published): The European Commission submitted a proposal for a Regulation on 27 October 2026 to digitalise case management for the return, readmission and reintegration of third-country nationals staying illegally in the European Union (EU). The proposal is subject to the ordinary legislative procedure; the European Parliament acts as co-legislator with the Council. It aims to support implementation of the common EU return system established by Regulation (EU) 2026/2280, reduce administrative workload and improve data sharing, automation and statistics.

Digital case-management architecture

The proposal provides for an integrated but partly decentralised digital architecture comprising:

national return case-management systems (national RECAMAS) in every Member State;

an integrated Union readmission case-management system (iRCMS);

a Union reintegration case-management system (RIAT);

the European Search Portal (ESP), the Central Repository for Reporting and Statistics (CRRS), and a secure communication portal.

Member States would develop national RECAMAS using the Frontex RECAMAS Model, a Frontex reference IT implementation, or a combination of both. National systems could remain centralised or federated and would be connected, where permitted by national law, to relevant national systems, including asylum, judicial, criminal-information, fingerprint-identification and flight-booking systems.

The iRCMS and RIAT would be integrated into national RECAMAS. The proposal provides for the systematic use of the ESP to access relevant EU information systems, within existing access rights. The communication portal would connect national RECAMAS and Frontex’s Integrated Return Management Application, enabling secure exchanges where necessary information is unavailable through EU information systems.

Functions and data exchanges

The proposed systems would support the management of return, readmission and reintegration cases through a single identifier for each person and automated case creation, data retrieval, deadline monitoring, case-status tracking and reporting. They would support secure digital communication and document exchange with third-country nationals, including through a dedicated mobile application, as well as the organisation of return operations and flight bookings.

National RECAMAS would support registration, initial and vulnerability checks, identity and nationality verification, preparation and notification of return and removal decisions, and management of measures to prevent absconding, detention and alternatives to detention. They would also support automated creation and updating of return alerts and European Return Orders in the Schengen Information System (SIS).

The iRCMS would support automated initiation of readmission procedures and applications, secure exchanges with third-country authorities, identification interviews, nationality verification, emergency travel documents and flight management. Connections with third-country digital systems could be established by Frontex, at the Commission’s request and where a relevant working arrangement permits digital exchanges. Third countries would have access only to data exchanged for an individual readmission case.

The RIAT would support financial management, quality assurance and monitoring of reintegration assistance. It would enable cooperation with third parties providing reintegration assistance, including the management of complaints, incidents, referrals and performance indicators.

Data protection, security and statistics

Competent authorities would record only data necessary for the relevant procedures and would be responsible for its accuracy and updating. They would normally access only data they recorded. Where multiple cases concerning the same person arise in different Member States, relevant data would be exchanged upon request through the communication portal where appropriate.

The proposal provides for rectification or erasure without delay of inaccurate, incomplete or unlawfully recorded data. Data would generally be retained for ten years after confirmation of return, or for the duration of an entry ban if longer; data linked to annulled or withdrawn return decisions would be deleted immediately. Processing would be subject to the General Data Protection Regulation and the EU data-protection rules for Union institutions and bodies.

It provides for encryption, access controls, individual user profiles, audit logs, recovery measures and automatic detection within 24 hours of events indicating a security incident. Logs would normally be retained for three years. Member States would designate data controllers for national RECAMAS and iRCMS; specific joint-controller and processor arrangements are proposed for RIAT and the communication portal.

Anonymised data, including case-status data, would be transferred automatically to the CRRS to produce statistics accessible to competent authorities, Frontex, the European Union Agency for the Operational Management of Large-Scale IT Systems in the Area of Freedom, Security and Justice (eu-LISA), and the Commission.

Governance, implementation and financing

Frontex would develop the RECAMAS Model, the Frontex RECAMAS, iRCMS and RIAT, and would operate iRCMS and RIAT. Member States would develop and operate national RECAMAS and integrate the Union-level systems. eu-LISA would develop and operate the communication portal and support Frontex in developing the other systems.

A ten-member Programme Management Board and a Frontex-led Advisory Group would coordinate development and technical evolution. Member States would be required to establish national RECAMAS within four years of the Regulation’s entry into force. The Commission would determine, by implementing acts following testing and successful integration, the dates on which iRCMS, RIAT and the communication portal would enter into operation.

The proposal would amend Regulations (EU) 2018/1726, 2018/1860, 2019/817 and 2019/818 to extend eu-LISA’s tasks, permit retention of relevant SIS return-alert data in national RECAMAS, use the communication portal for specified interoperability communications, and connect the CRRS to the new systems.

Indicative EU expenditure for 2028–2034 totals EUR 224.449 million, including EUR 156.054 million for Frontex, EUR 65.343 million for eu-LISA and EUR 3.052 million for Commission administrative expenditure. These amounts remain subject to agreement on the 2028–2034 Multiannual Financial Framework and annual budgetary procedures.

The European Commission proposed a new law on 27 October 2026 to move the handling of cases involving people staying illegally in the EU onto digital systems. These cases cover returning people to their home countries, readmitting them, and helping them resettle. The aim is to support the common EU return system set up by Regulation (EU) 2026/2280, cut the paperwork burden, and make data sharing, automation and statistics work better. The proposal will be considered through the ordinary EU law-making process, with the European Parliament and the Council acting as joint lawmakers.

The plan sets up a connected but partly decentralised set of digital tools. Every EU country would have its own national case-management system for returns, and there would be shared EU systems for readmission and reintegration. A search portal, a central database for reporting and statistics, and a secure messaging portal would link them together. Countries could build their national systems using a reference model from Frontex, the EU border agency, or a mix of that model and their own design, and they could link these to other national systems such as those for asylum, courts, criminal records, fingerprints and flight bookings, where their national law allows.

The systems would automate much of the work. Each person would have a single identifier, and the systems would create cases, pull up data, track deadlines and case status, and produce reports. They would also support secure digital communication with the people involved, including through a mobile app, and help arrange return flights. Third countries would only see data relating to a specific readmission case, and only where a working arrangement allows digital exchange.

On data protection, authorities would record only the information needed for each procedure and would be responsible for keeping it accurate. Inaccurate or unlawfully recorded data would have to be corrected or erased without delay. Data would generally be kept for ten years after a return is confirmed, or for as long as an entry ban lasts if that is longer, while data tied to cancelled or withdrawn return decisions would be deleted straight away. The systems would use encryption, access controls, audit logs and automatic detection of security incidents within 24 hours, and would follow the EU's General Data Protection Regulation and rules for EU bodies. Anonymised data would feed into a central statistics system.
